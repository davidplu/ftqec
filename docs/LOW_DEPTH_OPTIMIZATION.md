# Low-depth group-term optimization

`ftqec optimize code.json --memory-rounds 4 --target-distance 4 --population 12 --generations 1 --workers 2 --no-time-limits --output runs/2bga`

This opt-in driver uses the shared Task supervisor, fault screening, simulation, exact portfolio, Pareto routine, and public export. It generates term-order populations from the supplied multiplication table. Its initial serial X-then-Z candidate is code-derived, not a published reference schedule. Structured axis orders, random interleavings, parent term swaps, and witness-position term swaps create descendants. Signed instrument flows reject harmful noiseless interleavings. The driver also compiles the existing generic bare, flagged, cat and verified-cat gadgets with dedicated or reused ancilla slots and critical-path, conflict-color or resource scheduling. A repair-family finalist is retained alongside the shallowest candidates; extra protection is not rejected solely for increasing depth. This is a development allocation policy, not a demonstrated optimal policy.

Physical qubit dependencies determine actual schedule depth. Both Pauli memory sectors are simulated and certified separately. Both must be certified before the architecture enters the paired frontier. Sector frontiers remain separate because preparation/readout tasks differ. The driver retains all circuits, proposals, failures and incomplete proofs, and resumes source/config-bound checkpoints. Omit generations for anytime operation; cancellation and memory guards remain active.

`--memory-noise` sets the operation-local probability (default 0.001); `--memory-shots` sets initial shots (default 128). Selected finalists receive a separate four-times-larger batch with a distinct seed. Both batches are retained; they are not silently combined or presented as a matched-compute decoder comparison. `--decoder` and `--proof-method` are forwarded to the shared evaluators. Unsupported matching decomposition is recorded explicitly; automatic fallback is a weak one-mechanism lookup decoder. Decoder failures do not invalidate a circuit.

Uniform term ordering does not by itself prove a non-abelian fault-orbit symmetry. No orbit pruning or reference-schedule seeding is used. A shallow schedule is not automatically fault tolerant. Comparisons must count explicit H/reset/readout costs consistently; published depth conventions may use native X-basis operations absent from this gate set.
